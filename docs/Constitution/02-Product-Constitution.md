=================================================================
PURPOSE
=================================================================

This document defines how ATI must be experienced.
Not how it is implemented.
Not how it is architected.
Not how it is coded.
But how it feels to the operator and to anyone who must trust its decisions.

This is a trading intelligence. The primary user is the operator (human) who carries ultimate authority and accountability for capital. The product exists to make the operator's decisions better, safer, and more informed — and to execute disciplined decisions the operator has approved.

Every engineering decision must ultimately improve the product experience.
The operator never buys architecture.
The operator experiences the product.

=================================================================
PRODUCT IDENTITY
=================================================================

ATI IS
• a disciplined trading partner
• a persistent market intelligence
• an explainable decision system
• a risk guardrail
• a learning system with a track record
• a research environment
• a planning environment
• a review environment
• a decision-support environment

ATI IS NOT
• a chat application
• a prompt interface
• a model launcher
• a provider selector
• a dashboard of features
• an AI showcase
• a technology demonstration
• an execution runtime
• a black box that trades without explanation

ATI is a product.
Everything else serves the product.

=================================================================
PRODUCT MISSION
=================================================================

Enable the operator to
• understand markets more deeply
• make better decisions
• reduce cognitive load
• increase confidence
• maintain context across sessions
• know what the system is doing and why
• delegate disciplined execution to software
• remain the ultimate safety authority

ATI should increase decision quality, not simply execution speed.

=================================================================
PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
=================================================================

Always Optimize For
• trust
• clarity
• confidence
• transparency
• predictability
• discipline
• continuity
• simplicity
• explainability
• reliability
• safety

Never Optimize For
• visual complexity
• feature quantity
• novelty
• engineering ego
• hidden behavior
• unnecessary customization
• unnecessary settings
• unnecessary interruptions
• unnecessary AI interactions

=================================================================
THE OPERATOR IS THE CENTER
=================================================================

Every decision begins with
• "What does the operator actually need to decide or verify?"

Not
• "What can the architecture do?"
• "What does the AI support?"
• "What technology do we want to use?"

The product exists for the operator.
Technology serves the product.
The product serves the operator.

=================================================================
OPERATOR GOALS
=================================================================

Operators come to ATI to
• understand market state
• review what the system observes
• review why the system reasons the way it does
• approve or reject proposed decisions
• set risk boundaries
• review performance and learning
• maintain continuity of context
• audit everything the system has done

Everything else is secondary.

=================================================================
PRODUCT FLOW
=================================================================

ATI should naturally guide the operator through
• Open ATI
• See current market context
• See what ATI observes
• See why ATI believes what it believes
• See the current decision proposal with evidence
• Approve or reject
• See execution within risk bounds
• See outcome
• See what was learned
• Continue tomorrow

This flow should feel inevitable.
Never forced.
Never confusing.

=================================================================
TRUST
=================================================================

Trust is ATI's most valuable asset.
Trust is earned through
• predictability
• transparency
• correctness
• explanation
• consistency
• recoverability

Trust is lost through
• hidden actions
• silent failures
• unexplained behavior
• random AI decisions
• inconsistent interfaces
• broken workflows
• surprising execution

Every feature should increase trust.

For a trading system, trust has a special meaning: the operator must be able to explain every decision the system made, after the fact, to an auditor. If the system cannot produce that explanation, the decision should not have been executed.

=================================================================
DECISION TRANSPARENCY
=================================================================

ATI should explain
• what it observes
• what it infers and why
• what it proposes and with what confidence
• why a proposal was accepted or rejected by risk
• why execution paused, failed, or recovered
• what the outcome was
• what was learned

Operators should never need to guess.

Every important decision carries: evidence, confidence, uncertainty, and rationale — per the Decision Proposal schema (Document 05).

=================================================================
SIMPLICITY
=================================================================

• Complex systems may exist internally.
• Simple systems must exist externally.
• Operators should never carry internal complexity.
• Complexity belongs inside ATI.
• Not inside the operator's head.

=================================================================
PRODUCT VOCABULARY
=================================================================

Operators should primarily encounter words like
• Market Context
• Observation
• Decision Proposal
• Evidence
• Confidence
• Risk
• Execution
• Outcome
• Ledger
• Lesson
• Approval

Avoid exposing
• adapter
• broker
• bus
• provider
• capability
• event loop
• orchestration engine
• dependency injection
• transport layer

Those belong inside ATI.

=================================================================
INFORMATION HIERARCHY
=================================================================

Primary
• Current market context
• Current decision proposal
• Current risk status
• Current position/execution state
• Pending approvals

Secondary
• History
• Research
• Performance
• Lessons learned
• Documentation

Advanced
• Providers
• Integrations
• Diagnostics
• Logs
• Internal state

The operator should naturally spend almost all of their time in the primary layer.

=================================================================
FAILURE AND SAFETY UX
=================================================================

• Failures are loud, structured, and explained.
• A risk trip is shown as a protective event, not a bug.
• Recovery is always explainable.
• The operator always knows: where am I, what happened, what happens next.
• A rejected proposal shows exactly which risk constraint rejected it.

=================================================================
FIRST-TIME EXPERIENCE
=================================================================

• First launch should communicate: Welcome, configure risk boundaries, watch the system observe, see its first explanations.
• Do not overwhelm.
• Do not expose advanced systems.
• Do not expose internal architecture.
• Progressive disclosure.

=================================================================
LONG-TERM EXPERIENCE
=================================================================

• After months of use, ATI should feel like a familiar, disciplined trading partner.
• Not software.
• Not a toy.
• The operator should gradually trust its judgment while always retaining the final word.

=================================================================
PRODUCT REVIEW QUESTIONS
=================================================================

Every feature must answer
• Does this improve decision quality?
• Does this improve safety?
• Does this improve trust?
• Does this improve clarity?
• Does this improve continuity?
• Does this simplify the workflow?
• Would the operator miss it if removed?
• Does it feel like ATI?

=================================================================
PRODUCT SUCCESS METRICS
=================================================================

Success is measured by
• fewer unexplained decisions
• faster operator understanding
• higher decision quality over time
• lower cognitive load
• stronger session continuity
• better risk adherence
• higher operator confidence
• more auditable behaviour

Not by
• number of features
• number of integrations
• number of venues
• number of models
• number of indicators

=================================================================
PRODUCT ANTI-PATTERNS
=================================================================

Avoid becoming
• a settings application
• a dashboard of technologies
• a model comparison tool
• a venue marketplace
• a command launcher
• a chat app pretending to be a trading terminal
• a black-box autopilot

ATI is one cohesive product.

=================================================================
DEFINITION OF SUCCESS
=================================================================

ATI succeeds when
• the operator thinks about markets, not about the software
• every decision can be explained with evidence
• risk discipline is never compromised for a return
• the operator trusts the system's judgment
• the system learns measurably from outcomes
• trading feels calmer, clearer, and more intentional

=================================================================
CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE
=================================================================

ATI is not competing to have the most features.
ATI is competing to provide the best, safest, most explainable trading decisions.
Every decision must strengthen that experience.
