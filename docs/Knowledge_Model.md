# Knowledge Model

# Knowledge, Memory, and Learning in ATI

---

# Purpose

This document defines what ATI knows, remembers, learns, and believes.

Knowledge is not memory.

Memory is not experience.

Experience is not understanding.

Separating these concepts creates a more stable and explainable intelligence.

---

# Knowledge Hierarchy

ATI organizes information into five distinct layers.

Reality

↓

Observations

↓

Knowledge

↓

Experience

↓

Wisdom

Each layer builds upon the previous one.

---

# Reality

Reality consists of everything that exists outside ATI.

Examples:

- market prices
- exchange events
- economic news
- executed orders
- timestamps
- order books

Reality exists independently of ATI.

ATI cannot change reality.

It can only observe it.

---

# Observations

Observations are raw information collected from reality.

Examples:

BTC moved 2%.

Volume increased.

Funding became positive.

Volatility expanded.

An order failed.

Observations should contain no interpretation.

They simply describe what happened.

---

# Knowledge

Knowledge consists of stable concepts about markets.

Knowledge changes very slowly.

Examples:

Markets alternate between expansion and contraction.

Liquidity attracts participation.

High volatility increases uncertainty.

Risk compounds over repeated losses.

Knowledge should remain stable across market cycles.

Knowledge is not tied to individual trades.

---

# Experience

Experience connects knowledge with real outcomes.

Examples:

A liquidity sweep followed by strong buying often preceded profitable entries in similar conditions.

During highly volatile news events, execution quality decreased.

These are experiences.

They are neither universal truths nor isolated observations.

Experience grows over time.

---

# Wisdom

Wisdom is ATI's ability to apply knowledge and experience appropriately.

Wisdom answers questions such as:

Should I act?

Should I wait?

Should confidence be reduced?

Should risk be increased?

Wisdom emerges from repeated reflection.

It cannot be hardcoded.

---

# Memory Types

ATI maintains several independent memory systems.

---

## Working Memory

Current observations.

Current hypotheses.

Current reasoning.

Current decisions.

Working memory is temporary.

---

## Episodic Memory

Individual trading sessions.

Individual decisions.

Individual mistakes.

Individual successes.

This memory answers:

"What happened?"

---

## Semantic Memory

General market knowledge.

Stable concepts.

Definitions.

Relationships.

This memory answers:

"What is true?"

---

## Procedural Memory

How ATI performs tasks.

Examples:

How to execute orders.

How to evaluate confidence.

How to calculate risk.

How to analyze market state.

Procedural memory improves execution quality.

---

## Reflective Memory

Lessons learned.

Reasoning improvements.

Recurring mistakes.

Successful adaptations.

Reflection should update this memory.

---

# Beliefs

ATI maintains beliefs rather than certainties.

Examples:

Current trend is likely bullish.

Momentum appears weakening.

Liquidity may exist above recent highs.

Beliefs should always include confidence.

Beliefs change when evidence changes.

---

# Confidence

Confidence is attached to every important belief.

High confidence requires strong supporting evidence.

Low confidence reflects uncertainty.

Confidence should evolve continuously.

ATI should avoid binary thinking.

---

# Forgetting

Not all information deserves permanent storage.

ATI should gradually forget:

Outdated market conditions.

Temporary anomalies.

Noise.

Duplicate experiences.

Rare coincidences.

Useful knowledge should remain.

Noise should disappear.

---

# Learning

Learning should improve:

Knowledge quality.

Confidence calibration.

Decision quality.

Risk assessment.

Execution quality.

Learning should never overwrite stable knowledge without substantial evidence.

---

# Explainability

ATI should always explain:

What do I know?

What do I merely suspect?

What have I experienced?

What evidence supports this?

How confident am I?

Why?

Every important belief should be traceable.

---

# Final Principle

Knowledge provides understanding.

Experience provides context.

Wisdom provides judgment.

ATI becomes more intelligent not by storing more information, but by improving how it transforms information into better decisions.
